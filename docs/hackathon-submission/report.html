Part IProject overview
01 Executive summary

A second pair of eyes on every ambulance run

SwiftCare GeoAgent watches an ambulance's GPS trail against the road corridor it was sent on. When the vehicle drifts, slows or runs into a reported incident, the system works out what that means for arrival time, compares the alternatives, and puts a clear recommendation in front of a control-room operator. The operator approves or rejects it. The software never changes a mission on its own.

80backend endpoints, all role-checked except health and route preview
12read-only tools the AI assistant can call
16auditable reason codes behind every decision
86/86auth and access-control checks passing

What exists today

  • A deployed web application (Next.js on Vercel) with separate workspaces for control-room operators, ambulance drivers, paramedics and administrators, behind real login and admin approval, and live updates over Socket.IO.
  • An Express and MongoDB backend on Render with 17 modules covering vehicles, emergencies, incidents, routes, telemetry, deviation, traffic, prediction, decisions, AI orchestration, corridor clearance, SMS and administration.
  • Road-following routes from OSRM (the OpenStreetMap routing engine), deviation bands at 50, 100 and 250 metres, and a delay model that labels each input as observed, derived, inferred or unknown.
  • A deterministic decision engine that produces one of five actions with reason codes, stores a full input snapshot, and requires operator approval before execution. The engine and approval lifecycle are tested on the backend; in the deployed web app the approve, reject and execute buttons cannot reach it yet because their proxy routes are missing (Section 06).
  • GeoAgent, an optional AI layer that may only read data through 12 tools, uses free models only, and falls back to a rule-based answer when no model is available. In the live deployment no model key is configured, so the fallback is what runs.

Abstract

Ambulance crews and dispatchers lose minutes to wrong turns, unreported congestion and late decisions about sending a second unit. SwiftCare GeoAgent is a prototype decision-support system that turns raw position updates into corridor deviation states, delay forecasts and route comparisons, then applies a fixed, readable rule set to recommend continuing, rerouting, considering a backup unit or alerting the control room. An optional language-model assistant explains the situation using read-only tools but cannot override the rules, and mission-changing actions pass through an audited approval step. The system is deployed as a Next.js frontend on Vercel and an Express/MongoDB backend on Render, and is demonstrated on seeded Bengaluru scenarios with mock traffic and a simulated V2X (vehicle-to-everything) signal corridor.

02 Problem statement

The problem and why it matters

In an emergency the route plan is made once, at dispatch. After that, the road changes and the plan does not. (This prototype is not connected to any real emergency service, signal controller or patient record; see Section 03 for scope.)

What goes wrong on a typical run

Drift

Off the planned road

A missed turn or a forced detour puts the ambulance on a road the plan never considered. The dispatcher only finds out when the crew calls in, or when the ETA quietly slips.

Delay

Congestion and incidents

A crash or road closure ahead can add several minutes. The information often exists somewhere, but it is not tied to the specific vehicle and route that it affects.

Escalation

Backup decided too late

Deciding whether a closer unit should also go is a judgement call made under pressure, usually without a side-by-side comparison of arrival times.

Why it matters

For time-critical conditions such as cardiac arrest, stroke and major trauma, minutes of delay are widely recognised as affecting outcomes. This project does not claim a measured clinical benefit; it has not been evaluated with real crews. The engineering goal is narrower and testable: notice a problem on the road as soon as the data shows it, explain it, and give a person a safe, auditable choice.

03 Proposed solution

Solution, users and scope

The system runs a short loop for every active mission: take a position, compare it with the corridor, update the delay forecast, apply the rules, and ask a human when an action is needed.

The core loop

  1. Position in. A GPS point is stored as a trajectory record (source: device, simulator or API) and snapped to the planned route geometry.
  2. Corridor check. Distance and heading from the route give a deviation state: on route, warning, deviated or critical.
  3. Forecast. A background job updates predicted ETA and delay for that vehicle, with each factor's evidence type recorded.
  4. Rules. When analysis is requested, the decision engine combines deviation, traffic, delay, incidents, alternative routes and available backups into one recommended action.
  5. Explain. GeoAgent (if configured) gathers the same facts through read-only tools and writes a short explanation. Disagreement with the rules is flagged, not acted on.
  6. Approve. The operator approves or rejects. Only an approved decision can be executed, and a reroute is executed only if the approved candidate route has not changed since.

Intended users

RoleWhat they use the system forLanding page

Scope and boundaries

In scope
  • Monitoring missions that already exist in the system
  • Road routing, deviation, delay forecast, route comparison
  • Rule-based recommendations with approval and audit
  • Role-based access and live dashboards
  • Simulated corridor clearance to show the V2X idea
Out of scope
  • Receiving real emergency calls or dispatching real vehicles
  • Medical triage or diagnosis
  • Controlling real traffic signals
  • Live commercial traffic feeds (supported in code, not enabled)
  • Voice transmission between crews

Key differentiators

  • Rules decide, AI explains. The language model cannot write data or change the outcome. If it disagrees, the decision carries an AI_RECOMMENDATION_CONFLICT reason code for the operator to see.
  • Honest about uncertainty. Prediction factors and agent facts carry an evidence type. Missing data produces INSUFFICIENT_DATA and an alert, never a guess.
  • Approval that cannot go stale. Executing a reroute re-checks that the approved candidate is unchanged; otherwise it returns HTTP 409 and asks for fresh analysis.
  • Works with zero paid services. OSRM routing, mock traffic, free-only model selection and a JavaScript fallback for the Python engine.

Known gap in the deployed app

The browser only calls same-origin routes under app/api/, which forward to Express. Several backend endpoints that the UI uses have no such forwarding route yet, so in the deployed app they answer 404 or 405 even though the backend behind them works and is protected:

  • decision approve, reject and execute;
  • run analysis (orchestration) and task-routing recommendations;
  • manual override, assign unit and driver reroute;
  • driver incident report (POST) and full trail history.

This was confirmed by probing the live site on 10 October 2026. Each fix is a small file using the existing proxyToBackend helper, and it is the top item on the roadmap.

Emergency management

An emergency has a type (MEDICAL, ACCIDENT, FIRE, POLICE, OTHER), a priority (CRITICAL, HIGH, MEDIUM, LOW), a GeoJSON location with a geospatial index, an optional destination hospital and an assigned vehicle. Control-room users and admins create and update emergencies; only admins can delete, and deletes are soft (isDeleted). Drivers and paramedics can read them. Each emergency also keeps a communication block for the status-SMS feature.

Vehicle tracking and status

Vehicles carry an ID, registration, type, status, crew and hospital details. Drivers and paramedics may only read or update the vehicle assigned to them; this is enforced on the server by requireVehicleOwnership, not only hidden in the interface. The most recent trajectory point and the recent trail are fetched for the map and for deviation checks.

Incident management

Incidents (accident, road closure, road work, traffic jam, heavy traffic, breakdown, lane blockage and others) have a severity, a status (ACTIVE, RESOLVED, DISMISSED) and a location with a geospatial index. Any signed-in role can report one. An ACTIVE CRITICAL incident correlated with a vehicle's corridor makes the decision engine treat the road as blocked.

Illustrative exampleA driver reports a lane blockage near a flyover. The incident is saved with its coordinates and broadcast to the control room. On the next analysis for the ambulance using that road, the rules add CRITICAL_INCIDENT and ROAD_BLOCKED, look for an alternative that saves at least two minutes, and raise a REROUTE decision for the operator, or an alert if no alternative qualifies.
07 Routing and monitoring

Routing, ETA, traffic and deviation

This is the data path that turns a GPS point into a decision input. Each stage says where its data comes from.

Route planning

routing.service.js picks the provider at request time. Every returned route is validated before use: it must be a GeoJSON LineString with at least two coordinates and numeric distance and duration, which rejects straight-line placeholders. Routes are stored with their provider (MOCK, GOOGLE, MAPBOX, OSRM) and a type (PLANNED, ALTERNATIVE, CURRENT, RECOMMENDED, HISTORICAL), so the history of a mission's routing is kept.

ETA and traffic analysis

The prediction service produces predicted and baseline ETA, delay in seconds and minutes, a delay risk and a route risk (LOW to CRITICAL), a confidence level, and whether a reroute is advised with an urgency from NONE to IMMEDIATE. Each contributing factor is tagged OBSERVED (measured), DERIVED (calculated from measurements), INFERRED (estimated) or UNKNOWN, and the traffic source is recorded. With the mock traffic provider live, traffic-based factors in the deployed demo are not real-world measurements.

08 GeoAgent AI

GeoAgent: an assistant that can only read

GeoAgent writes a plain-language situation summary and a suggested action for the operator. It gathers facts by calling tools that read the database; none of its tools can write.

The 12 tools

getEmergencyStategetTrafficAnalysis
getVehicleStategetPrediction
getRecentTrajectorygetNearbyIncidents
getCurrentRoutegetDecisionHistory
getRouteAlternativesgetVehicleSituation
getNearbyAvailableVehiclesgetCorridorGreenWaveStatus

Each call is scoped to the emergency or vehicle under analysis, with caps such as 100 trajectory points, 5 km incident radius, 10 km backup radius and 50 decision records.

How uncertainty is handled

  • The agent keeps a record of what it knows; each fact is OBSERVED or DERIVED, and anything it could not fetch stays UNKNOWN rather than being filled in.
  • Answers with confidence below 0.5 are marked as needing operator review.
  • A route is called materially better only if it saves at least 2 minutes, calculated in code, not by the model.
  • A repeated identical tool call returns the cached result instead of running again.

Provider selection and failover

Live status: no OpenRouter or OpenCode key is set on Render, so every GeoAgent request in the deployed app returns the deterministic fallback, clearly marked as such. The model path is covered by 25 passing unit tests with mocked providers; it was not exercised against a live model during this audit.

09 Decisions

Decision recommendations and operator approval

A decision is a stored record: the recommended actions, the reasons, a snapshot of every input, and who did what to it and when. Recommending and executing are separate steps owned by different parties.

The rules, in order

#ConditionEffect
1Emergency or vehicle missingALERT_CONTROL_ROOM, CRITICAL, INSUFFICIENT_DATA
2Vehicle not operational / route not ACTIVEAlert; CRITICAL / WARNING
3DEVIATED or CRITICAL_DEVIATION; HEAVY or SEVERE traffic; ACTIVE CRITICAL incident on corridor; V2X corridor blockedMarks "needs reroute" with reason codes
4Score each alternative: ETA + traffic weight (0–4) + incident weight (0–3)Lowest score that saves ≥ 2 min wins
5Needs reroute and a viable alternative existsREROUTE, ALTERNATIVE_ROUTE_AVAILABLE
6Needs reroute, no viable alternativeALERT_CONTROL_ROOM, ROAD_BLOCKED (no invented route)
7HIGH/CRITICAL priority and (ETA > 15 min or delay > 8 min) and a backup ≥ 5 min faster within 10 kmCONSIDER_BACKUP
8None of the aboveCONTINUE, OPERATIONAL_BASELINE
9GeoAgent suggests something elseAdds AI_RECOMMENDATION_CONFLICT; result unchanged

When several actions apply, the primary one follows a fixed order: ALERT_CONTROL_ROOM, then REROUTE, CONSIDER_BACKUP, CONTINUE, NO_ACTION. Thresholds are prototype policy values and are documented in code as not medically validated.

What "execute" actually does

ActionSide effect on execution
ALERT_CONTROL_ROOMEmits a decision event to the control room.
REROUTEActivates the approved candidate route, but only if the approved candidate ID matches and its content is unchanged; otherwise HTTP 409 "reanalyze and approve again".
CONSIDER_BACKUPRecords the recommendation. It does not dispatch a second vehicle.
CONTINUE / NO_ACTIONNo side effect; the outcome is logged.

Deployment status: everything in this section runs and is tested on the backend. The deployed control room cannot call approve, reject or execute yet, because the Vercel proxy routes for those three endpoints were never added. Until they are, approval can only be demonstrated against the backend directly or on a local setup.

10 Other workflows

Driver, paramedic, admin, voice and degraded states

Driver workflow

The driver dashboard is a navigation display for the assigned vehicle: the active route leg, the next manoeuvre, distance remaining and route status. Route steps are normalised by the navigation engine in lib/navigation (11 unit tests). The phone's own GPS (browser geolocation) positions the driver on the map. In the current build that position is used on screen only; it is not uploaded as telemetry. Telemetry in the deployed demo comes from seeded trajectories and the playback script. The "reroute from current position" action and the driver's incident-report form both call backend endpoints that are not yet proxied (Section 06).

Paramedic workflow

The paramedic page shows the assigned unit's mission, destination and status in a layout aimed at patient handoff. It reads the same mission data as the driver view. No patient data, vitals feed or hospital system is connected.

Admin and access control

New users register with a requested role and workspaces and start as PENDING. An administrator approves, rejects or suspends them and can change roles. The admin console also shows system statistics, provider health, prediction analytics, and a read-only database explorer for vehicles, emergencies, incidents, routes, trajectories, predictions, decisions and prediction analytics. Demo-data seed and reset endpoints are admin-only, and reset is blocked in production unless explicitly allowed.

Voice

The control room has a push-to-talk dialog: press and hold to record, with microphone-permission handling (granted, denied, prompt, unavailable) and a duration timer. No audio is sent anywhere; the dialog says so. Real voice would need WebRTC signalling and STUN/TURN servers. Simulated

Part IIISystem architecture
11 Architecture

High-level architecture

Frontend architecture

The frontend is a Next.js 16.4 App Router application in TypeScript with React 19, Tailwind CSS 4 and shadcn-style components. Pages are protected with a client-side ProtectedRoute that reads the session and a role-to-page map. All data calls go to same-origin routes under /api/*. This backend-for-frontend (BFF) layer forwards to the Express backend using proxyToBackend, adds the user's credentials, applies a timeout, and keeps the backend address on the server. Because the browser only talks to its own origin, the session cookie can stay HttpOnly and there is no cross-site cookie to manage for normal requests.

Backend architecture

The backend is an Express 4 application in ES modules. Each domain lives in server/modules/<name> with the same files: routes, controller, service, validation and, where needed, a Mongoose model. Routes attach protect (verify the JWT) and requireRole; controllers handle HTTP; services hold logic and talk to models; a shared error handler turns operational errors into JSON with the right status code. Cross-module behaviour (telemetry → prediction, decision → reroute) goes through service calls, and real-time output goes through one realtimeService.

Database architecture

MongoDB was chosen because mission data is document-shaped (a route with its legs, steps and geometry; a decision with its full input snapshot) and because GeoJSON with 2dsphere indexes gives "what is near this point" queries without a separate GIS database.

13 Interfaces

APIs, real-time, providers, Python and V2X

API architecture

The backend exposes 80 method-and-path combinations under 14 /api routers, plus four health routes and a root probe. Every data router requires a valid session and a role; the exceptions are health checks, login/register, and POST /api/routes/calculate, which is public so the landing page can preview a route. Validation middleware checks IDs and payloads before controllers run. The Vercel layer exposes 35 proxy routes for the endpoints the UI uses. The full list is in Section 18.

Real-time communication

Socket.IO keeps a live connection between each dashboard and the backend, so a new position, decision or incident appears without reloading. The browser first asks the Vercel app for a short-lived socket token, then connects directly to Render. The server verifies the token, checks the role and joins the socket to rooms.

Python routing engine and V2X

The routing-engine/ folder holds the Python modules (Section 16). The backend calls the bridge with spawnSync (1.5 s timeout) to compute green-wave status for four demo signals on Bengaluru corridors (Mayo Hall junction, 100 ft Road, HAL 2nd Stage, Airport Road). If the script or Python is missing, an in-process JavaScript engine gives the same result. On Render the backend starts inside server/, so the script is not found and the JavaScript engine runs; the health endpoint reports this as DEGRADED. Corridor clearance sessions (simulated vehicles moving from DETECTED to CLEARED) are stored with isSimulated; nothing is sent to real vehicles or signals.

15 Workflows

How the main flows run

Emergency lifecycle, end to end

  1. Operator creates an emergency (PENDING).
  2. A vehicle is assigned (DISPATCHED); a route is calculated and stored with legs.
  3. Telemetry arrives; deviation and prediction update; dashboards follow live.
  4. Operator or orchestration requests analysis; the engine stores a decision.
  5. Operator approves or rejects; an approved reroute is executed and broadcast as route.updated.
  6. Vehicle reaches the scene (AT_SCENE), then the hospital leg; the emergency is RESOLVED.

Orchestrated analysis

POST /api/orchestration/emergencies/:id/analyze runs the whole chain in one call: gather the situation, run GeoAgent (or its fallback), evaluate the rules, store the decision and emit geoagent.analysis.created and decision.created. The response tells the operator what was observed, what was inferred, and what is waiting for approval.

Important implementation decisions

  • Same-origin proxy instead of calling Render from the browser: keeps the session cookie HttpOnly and the backend address private, at the cost of one proxy file per endpoint. Missing files are the cause of every deployed 404 listed in Section 06.
  • Rules in plain code, not in the model: every outcome can be traced to a line in decision.rules.js and a reason code.
  • Free models only, checked at call time: no surprise bills and no dependency on a paid key for the demo.
  • Background prediction after ingestion: keeps telemetry writes fast; predictions arrive over sockets shortly after.
  • JavaScript twin for the Python engine: the backend works on any Node host.

Validation and error handling

Each module has a validation file that checks IDs, coordinates, enums and payload shape before the controller runs. Mongoose schemas add a second layer (for example speed ≤ 250 km/h, heading 0–360). Operational errors carry a status code and are returned as JSON; unexpected errors become 500 without stack traces in production. 4xx responses are logged as warnings, 5xx as errors.

Logging and observability

  • Health endpoints for liveness, readiness (database ping) and provider state, used by Render's health check.
  • Tagged console logs per subsystem (for example [GeoAgent AI], [RoutingService]), with database URIs redacted.
  • Decisions store an input snapshot, so a past recommendation can be explained after the fact.
Part VISafety, security and reliability
20 Safety and security

Security, safeguards and failure modes

Authentication and authorisation

  • Passwords hashed with bcrypt; JWT sessions (7 days by default) in an HttpOnly cookie, SameSite=None; Secure in production.
  • New accounts are PENDING until an admin approves; suspended or rejected users cannot sign in.
  • Every data route checks role on the server; crews are limited to their assigned vehicle by ownership middleware.
  • Socket connections are authenticated and room joins re-checked.
  • Rate limits: login/register 30 per 15 min, AI analysis 30 per min, writes 120 per min.

Secrets management

  • Secrets live only in the Vercel and Render dashboards; .env files are git-ignored.
  • Gitleaks scans the working tree and the full history on every CI run.
  • At start-up the database URI is validated and redacted before logging; error messages name the problem, never the value. One exception was found: the production-reset guard in dbSafety.js writes the full URI into its refusal message (see Section 22).
  • The backend address is server-only on Vercel.
  • Atlas network access is an allow list, not open to every address.

Uncertain and stale data

Telemetry freshness matters: a vehicle whose last fix is old should not be judged on it. Predictions record their inputs and evidence types, the deviation check needs consistent recent fixes, and the AI agent marks anything it could not fetch as unknown. What the system does not yet do is show an explicit "data is N minutes old" warning on every panel; that is on the roadmap.

Simulated versus live

Live and realSimulated or mock
Accounts, roles and approvals · MongoDB data · OSRM road geometry and durations · deviation maths · decision engine and audit · Socket.IO updates Traffic levels (mock) · V2X signals and connected vehicles · telemetry in the demo (seeded and played back) · push-to-talk · emergency lab and scenario pages · AI answers (rule-based fallback live)

Privacy considerations

The demo holds no real patient data. Emergency records can hold a caller name and phone number for the SMS feature, so production use would need consent handling, retention rules for those fields and encryption at rest beyond Atlas defaults. Telemetry already expires after 90 days through a TTL index.

Operational risks

  • Thresholds (50/100/250 m, 15 min, 8 min, 5 min) are prototype values, not validated with emergency services.
  • Mock traffic means delay predictions in the demo do not reflect real roads.
  • The free Render instance sleeps; a cold start can delay the first request by about a minute, which is unacceptable for real operations.
  • OSRM's public server has no service guarantee; production would need a self-hosted instance or a paid provider.
  • POST /api/routes/calculate is public and not rate-limited separately.
  • If a destructive demo reset is attempted against the production database, the refusal message logged by dbSafety.js includes the full connection string. It should be redacted the same way as start-up logging.

22 Operations

CI, deployment and troubleshooting

CI pipeline

The CI workflow runs on every push and pull request. On commit d516074 (the merge of the phase 3–5 work into main) it succeeded.

  1. Secret scanning with Gitleaks 8.30.1 (working tree, then history against a baseline).
  2. Typecheck and Next.js build.
  3. Backend suites with a MongoDB 7 service: database safety and retention; auth and RBAC against a started server with per-run random passwords; routing, navigation and real-time.
  4. Docker build of the backend image.

A second workflow, deploy.yml, reports failure on every push. Its Cloud Run job is entirely commented out, so GitHub sees a workflow with no jobs. It does not affect Vercel or Render. Deleting or disabling the file removes the red mark.

Deployment process

  1. Merge to main. Vercel builds the frontend and moves the production alias when the build passes; a failed build leaves the previous version live.
  2. Render rebuilds the backend (cd server && npm ci) and starts it (node server.js); traffic switches when /api/health/ready returns 200.
  3. Check /api/health/ready on Render and /api/health on Vercel (should report backend: ready).

23 Evaluation

Status, limitations and roadmap

Major engineering achievements

  • A complete approval-gated decision pipeline on the backend, with reason codes, input snapshots, idempotency and a stale-approval check.
  • An AI agent that is bounded, read-only, free-model-only and still useful when no model is available.
  • Role and ownership enforcement on HTTP and on sockets, with 86 automated access checks passing.
  • A production deployment across Vercel, Render and Atlas with health, readiness and provider reporting.
  • Graceful fallback on every external dependency: routing, snapping, traffic, AI, Python.

What makes it distinctive

Most route-assistance demos either stop at drawing a route or hand control to a model. This one keeps the model in an advisory seat, puts readable rules in charge, and treats the operator's approval as a first-class, audited step. It also says plainly which inputs are real, which are mock and which are unknown.

Limitations

  • Mock traffic, simulated V2X, simulated voice; no live LLM in the deployment.
  • Eight UI actions, including decision approval, cannot reach the backend in the deployed app yet.
  • Not evaluated with real crews, dispatch data or clinical outcomes.
  • Free-tier hosting with cold starts.

Conclusion

SwiftCare GeoAgent shows that continuous route monitoring and decision support for ambulances can be built from open components, with the AI kept in an explanatory role and a person in charge of every change. The tracking, deviation, prediction and rules stages are implemented, deployed and tested. The core loop is implemented and tested on the backend; the deployed app still needs the missing proxy routes before an operator can approve a decision from the browser. That fix, followed by live traffic data, is the clearest next step.
